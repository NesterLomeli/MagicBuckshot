MAGIC? BUCKSHOT! (formerly "No More Kvass") - CHANGES FROM THE DESIGN DOCUMENT
==============================================================================
BIGGEST CHANGE: SETTING AND TONE
- The design doc described a gritty Eastern European setting. I dropped
  that entirely and re-themed the game as a high-fantasy parody: Skeeter,
  a hillbilly whose moonshine still blew open a portal, watches a fantasy
  kingdom's Royal Guard confiscate his moonshine as "witchcraft". He follows
  them through with Pa's pump shotgun and invades the kingdom to
  get it back. The fish-out-of-water joke (a shotgun against swords and
  spells) gave the game a stronger identity than the original setting and
  more room for humour.
- The three level layouts were planned as text grids first (walls, enemies,
  props), then built as scenes.
- Art direction changed from "muted, gritty" to chunky low-res pixel art in
  the spirit of Risk of Rain 1: ~16 px characters, one limited palette per
  level, pixel-perfect camera, bright colours reserved for danger.
- The pistol became a pump-action 12 gauge modeled on an MP-133: 4+1
  shells, 12 pellets in a wide fan. Every shot is followed by racking the
  pump (sound + the spent hull flies out), so the fire rate is set by the
  pump cycle. Reloading loads one shell at a time with the counter ticking
  up, and pressing fire mid-reload stops loading and shoots with whatever
  is already in the tube, like a real tube-fed shotgun. This replaces the
  12-round, 1.2 s magazine reload from the design doc.
  The gun script still supports a pistol via Inspector settings.
- Balance flipped to a power fantasy. The original doc had an even,
  one-hit-each fight; now the player is deliberately overpowered:
  * enemies have health instead of dying to any hit (knights 6, wizards
    3). Shotgun pellets have damage falloff (3 up close, 2 mid range, 1
    far), so one blast kills every knight inside the spread cone at close
    to mid range, and only far-away targets need a second blast. Non-lethal hits flash the enemy, shove it back and
    interrupt a knight's sword swing, so the shotgun can save you mid-
    wind-up;
  * killed enemies are thrown back by the blast and skid away, leaving a
    few smears of blood (kept small so it's punchy rather than brutal);
  * wizards' magic bolts are slow and big enough to sidestep on reaction.
  The challenge comes from numbers instead of enemy strength, so enemy
  counts went from 3 / 5 / 7 to 9 / 14 / 29, and wizard bolts were sped up
  slightly (still dodgeable on reaction).
  The player still dies in one hit, so getting surrounded is still deadly.
- New weapon not in the design doc: the Gatling Staff, found on level 2.
  It's a messy bundle of confiscated wizard staffs, uneven lengths,
  wrapped in crooked duct tape. Skeeter holds it at the hip like a chainsaw.
  It's full-auto magic that spins up from slow to 20 shots per second
  while you hold the trigger, with an 80-bolt charge and a slower reload.
  Weapons are now data assets (ScriptableObjects), each remembering its
  own loaded ammo; Q or 1/2 swaps between them.
- Levels renamed to follow the invasion: Factory -> The Castle Gate,
  Interchange (shopping complex) -> The Royal Market, Lighthouse -> The
  Wizard's Tower. The Market and the Tower were later re-laid out (see
  Market Stands and The King's Feast below).
- Two enemy types instead of one (plus the King on the last level), all
  sharing the same AI script:
  Knights charge in and attack with a sword. Each swing is telegraphed
  by a red wind-up during which the knight plants its feet and locks its
  facing, so the player can shoot it first or step out of the arc.
  Wizards keep their distance and fire slow, dodgeable magic bolts.
  Mixing the two forces the player to handle a rush up close while
  dodging fire from range, which suits the short-range shotgun.
- Level exits are magic portals.

KEPT AS DESIGNED
- Top-down 2D shooter, WASD movement, mouse aim, left click to fire.
- Limited magazine, unlimited reserve ammo, reload on R (now 5 shotgun
  shells loaded one at a time instead of 12 pistol rounds, see above).
- One hit kills the player. Space restarts after death. (Enemies now have
  health, see above.)
- Three levels in the same order (layouts and enemy counts changed, see
  above).
- Title screen, on-screen ammo counter, death and level-clear feedback, victory screen.
- Walls block movement and bullets.
- No background music; the soundscape is effects only (see Sound below).

CHANGES AND ADDITIONS

Gameplay
1. Gunshot awareness went from "possible" to a core mechanic. Every shot
   (player or enemy) emits a noise radius; enemies who hear it walk to the
   sound. Walls muffle the sound, so shooting inside a closed room is
   quieter than shooting in a corridor.
2. Enemies got a full state machine instead of "detect, move, fire":
   idle / patrol -> investigate -> chase -> search -> return to post.
3. Enemies have a vision cone, so players can sneak up behind them. Anyone
   right next to an enemy is noticed from any direction.
4. Enemies use A* grid pathfinding to move around walls and through doors
   when they chase or investigate, instead of walking straight into walls.
5. Enemies have a short reaction delay before their first shot so a skilled
   player can always shoot first. Re-spotting after peeking a corner has a
   shorter delay.
6. No auto-reload: firing on an empty magazine gives a dry click and a
   "RELOAD [R]" prompt, which makes reloading a deliberate decision.
7. Breakable props: barrels, potion shelves and mana crystals shatter when
   shot, leave debris or a glowing puddle, and make noise that draws
   enemies. Shooting a potion shelf to lure a knight away is a valid tactic.
8. Level exit: after the last enemy dies, an exit zone opens and the player
   walks out to finish the level (instead of the level ending instantly).
9. Enemies cannot shoot each other (no friendly fire).

Feedback / Feel
10. "!" icon when an enemy spots you, "?" icon when it hears something.
11. Hit-stop (a tiny freeze) on every kill, screen shake on shots and deaths.
12. Camera leans toward the mouse cursor so you can see where you're aiming.
13. Ejected shell casings, muzzle flashes, wall sparks, and blood/debris
    decals that stay on the floor for the rest of the level.
14. Reload progress bar; ammo counter turns red on your last shell.

Presentation / Humour
- Ending cutscene (not in the doc): after the Wizard's Tower, Skeeter wakes
  up face-down in the grass by his trailer and red pickup, panics that
  someone stole his moonshine... then realises he drank it all. Was the
  kingdom ever real? It's skippable, then the victory stats are shown.
- The ammo counter is a row of mismatched shotgun shells (a worn one, a
  dented one, one with a doodled face) that go dark as you fire. The Gatling
  Staff shows a number instead.
- Recoil and racking: the gun kicks back on every shot and again when the
  pump is racked, and spent hulls fly out to the right and bounce.
- Shotgun blasts shove Skeeter backwards a step, with a muzzle flash almost
  twice the size and a split-second warm white flash over the screen. The
  Gatling Staff only nudges him back gently while it fires.
- Heavier screen shake on shotgun blasts: a hard jolt backwards along the
  aim plus a rumble that fades out, instead of a flat jitter.
- Removed the yellow reload bar. The shotgun's reload is shown by the shells
  filling back in one by one; the Gatling Staff shows "RELOADING...".
- The weapon name ("PA'S PUMP SHOTGUN") sits above the shells in the Jersey 10
  pixel font with a drop shadow.
- The rest of the HUD (enemy count, hints, level title, death and level-clear
  messages) now uses Jersey 10 with a hard drop shadow. "YOU HAVE FALLEN" is
  red, "QUEST COMPLETE" gold. Skeeter's quips stay in
  plain yellow Jersey 10 (tried another font, the original read better).
- Gatling Staff ammo is shown as a belt of 40 little bolt icons (ice, arcane,
  star, matching the bolts it fires); each icon is 2 rounds and goes dark as
  they're spent.

NEW: SMOKE BREAK (Max Payne 3 style bullet time)
- Shift or right click: Skeeter puts a cigarette in his mouth and time
  eases down to 30% over a quarter second, with a white pop, a deep whoosh
  and a heartbeat thumping under everything. Every sound drops in pitch.
- Black cinematic letterbox bars slide in, the picture gets a magenta wash
  with deep blue edges, and Skeeter leaves blue, red, green and pink
  afterimages, like Max Payne 3's colour bleed.
- Skeeter still walks at full speed and his gun runs at 70% of normal
  speed (it keeps its own clock), so he out-guns the slowed world while his
  pellets streak through the air with visible trails.
- Every hit during bullet time sprays a cone of blood mist out the far side
  that hangs in the air; killing shots get a big burst and an extra splat.
- One cigarette, shown bottom right. It burns for about 5 real seconds, can
  be put out early to save the rest, and slowly grows back (~14 s).
- Works with the kill hit-stop: after the freeze, time returns to slow
  motion instead of normal speed while he's smoking.

NEW: THE KING'S FEAST (start of Level 3)
- The tower courtyard and moat were replaced by a feast hall. Skeeter starts
  in a small stone entry facing a huge iron-banded double door. One shot
  blows it apart (planks fly, deep boom, camera shake) and a title card
  reads "THE KING'S FEAST".
- Inside: a long banquet table with ten knights and wizards seated on
  chairs, and the King himself on a gold throne at the head of the table.
  The guests sit facing the table and don't move until they can see
  Skeeter, then the whole feast jumps up at once.
- The King is a new enemy: crown, ermine collar, red robe, goblet. He casts
  like a wizard but takes 14 hits and fires faster.
- Everything on the table breaks: roast turkey (drumsticks fly), goblets of
  wine (red splash), fruit bowls and berry pies. Chairs and the throne
  splinter too. The table itself is solid to walk into but bullets and
  sight pass over it, so you can shoot across it.
- The confiscated moonshine sits on a side table in the corner.
- The hall is packed with breakables: two more food-laden side tables with
  guests, eight candelabras (candles go flying), six suits of armour on
  stands (helmet and sword clatter off), wine casks (planks and a red
  flood), two treasure chests (a spray of gold coins, with its own coin
  sound), two pink celebration cakes (frosting cloud and cherries) and two
  ice sculptures of the King (shatter into shards and frost).

GATLING STAFF: HEAVIER AND HOTTER
- Every bolt now does 2 damage (the gold star 3): knights drop in ~3 bolts.
- Each bolt leaves a glowing trail in its colour and bursts into a little
  coloured poof where it hits.
- The barrel heats up as you fire and smokes in cyan, purple and gold,
  and keeps smoking for a few seconds after you let go.
- It's heavy: Skeeter walks at 70% speed while firing, gets shoved back
  steadily, and the camera rumbles and leans back. Bigger muzzle flash and
  a faint strobe.


NEW: MARKET STANDS (Level 2)
- Three new breakable two-tile stands: fruit (crates smash, fruit rolls away
  and leaves a squashed smear), sword racks (three swords spin off and skid
  across the floor) and potion stands (burst into a cloud of coloured smoke
  and leave a puddle). Each has its own synthesized sound
  (Tools/make_sounds_extra.py).
- The Royal Market was re-laid out as aisles: four rows of stands with an
  open avenue down the middle. Five knights are AMBUSHERS crouched behind the
  stands (darker, in the stall's shadow). They ignore gunfire and wait until
  Skeeter walks within ~4.5 tiles in plain view, or one of them is shot, then
  they all jump out at once (each one shouts to wake hidden knights within
  7 tiles). Two more lie in wait in the library.
- The back shop is an apothecary full of potion stands.
- The staff room is now a LIBRARY: bookshelves ring the Gatling Staff, which
  rests on a stone pedestal. Like an enchanting table, little rune letters
  and sparkles lift off the nearby shelves and swirl into the staff
  (Assets/Scripts/Effects/EnchantGlyphs.cs). Shot bookshelves burst into
  fluttering pages.

Sound
17. Pitch randomisation on every effect so repeated shots/footsteps don't
    sound identical. Separate sounds for: player and enemy gunshots, dry
    fire, magazine out/in, ricochets, shell drops, footsteps, enemy alert
    and confused barks, deaths, prop breaking, level clear, exit opening.
    Knights clank and grunt during their wind-up, so you can hear an
    attack coming, and wizards' spells zap, so you can tell their fire
    from your own gun.

Cut / not done
- Background music: left out on purpose, per the assignment guidance. Short
  banjo stings play only on level start, death, level clear and victory, so
  they never mask gameplay sounds.

Developer tools (not part of normal play)
- F1 skip level, F2 god mode, F3 restart level, F4 kill all enemies. These
  only work in the Unity Editor and Development builds and were used for
  testing and recording the video.
